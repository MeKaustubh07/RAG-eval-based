# Retrieval Evaluation Results

40 synthetic questions, single relevant chunk each, retrieve k=10.
Corpus: 3 fitness research PDFs + 3 IR explainer notes → 181 chunks.

## The evaluation-bias finding (why there are two tables)

Synthetic test questions are generated by an LLM that has just read the target
chunk. Version 1 of the prompt let the LLM reuse the chunk's rare vocabulary
("phosphocreatine resynthesis", "muscle carnosine"), which leaks the answer's
exact tokens into the question. BM25 matches tokens exactly, so it scored
artificially high — the metric was measuring vocabulary overlap, not retrieval
quality.

Version 2 of the prompt forces paraphrasing ("a real user has not read this
passage and does not know its jargon"). This is what a real query looks like.

The two tables below are the same strategies on the same corpus — only the
test-question style changed. BM25 collapses; dense/rerank hold up. That gap
*is* the vocabulary-mismatch problem that motivates dense retrieval.

### Table 1 — biased testset (questions echo source vocabulary)

| strategy | recall@1 | recall@5 | recall@10 | mrr | ndcg@10 | latency_ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 0.300 | 0.625 | 0.675 | 0.409 | 0.473 | 250.8 |
| bm25 | 0.525 | 0.825 | 0.925 | 0.643 | 0.711 | 0.5 |
| hybrid | 0.425 | 0.825 | 0.850 | 0.597 | 0.661 | 8.7 |
| rerank | 0.300 | 0.600 | 0.950 | 0.468 | 0.580 | 1956.1 |

BM25 looks best — an artifact of token leakage, not real skill.

### Table 2 — paraphrased testset (realistic queries)  ← headline result

| strategy | recall@1 | recall@5 | recall@10 | precision@5 | mrr | ndcg@10 | hit_rate@10 | avg_latency_ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 0.325 | 0.600 | 0.675 | 0.120 | 0.440 | 0.497 | 0.675 | 269.4 |
| bm25 | 0.250 | 0.450 | 0.525 | 0.090 | 0.345 | 0.388 | 0.525 | 0.6 |
| hybrid | 0.275 | 0.475 | 0.625 | 0.095 | 0.392 | 0.448 | 0.625 | 8.7 |
| rerank | 0.325 | 0.575 | 0.725 | 0.115 | 0.425 | 0.495 | 0.725 | 863.9 |

Under realistic queries: **BM25 recall@5 drops 0.825 → 0.450** (the leaked
advantage evaporates), **dense becomes the strongest single retriever**, and
**rerank gives the best recall@10 (0.725)** by pulling correct chunks into the
candidate set before diversity selection.

## Reading the numbers

- **BM25 collapse** proves the bias was real and quantifies it (~0.38 recall@5 swing).
- **Rerank wins recall@10 but not recall@1**: MMR trades top-1 ranking for
  diversity, which helps coverage but not this single-relevant-chunk metric.
  On multi-passage questions MMR would help more.
- **Absolute numbers are modest** because the corpus is small and questions are
  deliberately hard. The story is the *relative* movement between tables.
- **Latency**: BM25 sub-millisecond, dense ~270 ms (query embedding), rerank
  ~0.9 s (cross-encoder forward passes). Real precision/latency tradeoff.

## Honest next steps (measured, not guessed)

- Query expansion should lift dense/hybrid recall — directly attacks vocabulary
  mismatch. (Phase 7b)
- Human-written questions would remove all synthetic bias — the gold standard.
- Larger corpus would raise absolute numbers and separate strategies more cleanly.
